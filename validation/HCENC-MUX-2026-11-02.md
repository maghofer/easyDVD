# HCEnc mux frame preservation

2026-11-02. Windows, FFmpeg/FFprobe 9.0.2, HCEnc 0.28.0.2 and the source-built
AviSynth+ / FFMS2 runtime. Scratch files, intermediate media, queues and builds
are isolated in local scratch storage, with process-local TEMP/TMP there.
The user's GUI queue, work directory and source media were not modified.

## Reproduction and cause

The [performance sample](PERFORMANCE-2026-11-02.md) is 300 PAL frames / 12 seconds.
HCEnc was run once with the existing BEST/two-pass settings to retain its raw
MPEG-2 stream. FFprobe confirmed all 300 decoded frames in that stream.
Multiple mux commands then reused that same encoded stream, without video
re-encoding:

| Mux behavior | Decoded video frames | Video duration | Video start minus audio start |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Previous output `-t 12` | 297 | 11.88 s | 45.333 ms |
| Drain video to EOF, no output `-t` | 300 | 12.00 s | 45.333 ms |
| Drain to EOF + normalize initial video PTS/DTS | 300 | 12.00 s | 5.333 ms |
| Normalize PTS/DTS but retain output `-t 12` | 297 | 11.88 s | 5.333 ms |

The output duration cutoff truncates stream-copied MPEG-2 with reordered
pictures. The prepared lossless video already has the desired frame range;
applying another time cutoff during multiplexing is unnecessary. FFmpeg's
generated timestamps also start the raw PAL video at PTS 0.04 / DTS 0.00,
introducing an extra video-frame offset relative to audio.

The correction removes the output cutoff and uses padded audio with
`-shortest`, so the complete encoded video determines the end. The `setts`
bitstream filter subtracts the first video PTS from both PTS and DTS, preserving
their relative difference and B-picture decode order. The residual 5.333 ms
start difference is the 256-sample AC-3 encoder delay at 48 kHz.

Reference: FFmpeg documents [output duration limits and `-shortest`](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg.html#Main-options)
and [packet timestamp expressions in `setts`](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-bitstream-filters.html#setts).

## Changes

- The HCEnc mux stream-copies video through EOF, normalizes its initial
  timestamps, and retains stereo AC-3 at 448 kbps / 48 kHz, audio padding,
  selected-stream mapping, silence generation and DVD mux constraints.
- FFmpeg setup validates the `setts` capability, including its single-column
  capability-list format.
- HCEnc's completed output frame count is read from its report. FFmpeg mux
  progress must report the same count before a checkpoint can be published.
  This adds no video decoding or encoding pass. The existing complete decode
  and file hashing still follow.
- HCEnc quality, bitrate, quantization, GOP and field-order settings are
  unchanged. The MPEG-2 payload hash before/after the corrected direct mux is
  identical:
  `cc83ece1b9ed3d0c9c6e1e44548d3eeccf24c52fe5c718d536e5c8edf4f84154`.
- Quality uses a v2 cache identity. Earlier HCEnc v1 checkpoints in unfinished
  jobs may already be missing frames and must be regenerated on resume; missing
  picture data cannot be restored by remuxing. Balanced cache identity is
  unchanged. Completed ISO sets are not automatically rebuilt.

## Regression coverage

The dedicated mux regression exercises PAL and NTSC at 1, 2, 17, 53 and 300
frames. The 53-frame cases also cover short audio padded with silence, long
audio stopped at video EOF, and selecting an audible second audio track over
a silent first track. Every case checks:

- The exact decoded video frame count.
- An identical encoded-video payload SHA-256 before and after muxing.
- Audio/video start alignment within the AC-3 encoder delay and rounding.
- Audio end alignment within one 1536-sample AC-3 packet (32 ms), calculated
  from decoded frame counts rather than unreliable tiny-file duration estimates.

The full production PAL/NTSC pipeline checks now count decoded frames through
the DVD-Video ISO demuxer for Quality titles, including retained interlacing,
cross-standard conversion and silent sources. Each two-second Quality title
retains its expected 50 PAL or 60 NTSC frames. Pause/reopen/resume and compatible
checkpoint reuse continue to pass.

## Real sample through the production CLI

A fresh explicit queue processed the 12-second real-media sample using Quality,
one worker and no menu. It completed encoding, authoring and ISO publication.
The job log records `HCEnc frame preservation: 300 encoded / 300 muxed`.

| Artifact | Video frames | AC-3 frames | Video start | Audio start |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Encoded MPG | 300 | 375 | 0.540000 s | 0.534667 s |
| Authored title VOB | 300 | 375 | 0.540000 s | 0.534667 s |

The ISO's DVD-Video title also decodes to all 300 video frames. Both elementary
payloads are identical between the MPG and the authored VOB:

- Video: `c3368b041a6a0b2b5df3355669bd0c7b2f63362ed94a352ef542962247fa4d47`
- AC-3: `618a990c2ca00346c4f522bcc8a6b5f47098252fcb0a2edca2d5b231ae468d46`

ISO: 14,041,088 bytes, SHA-256
`01826a09f3f450c6c2914b982980d65731ec39a0f1a71598495ce8e7a32bd12e`.
The hash matches the published manifest.

FFmpeg's DVD demux/null-output path reports an audio timestamp warning at the
end of the authored sample; audio frame reporting through that demuxer differs
from raw VOB reporting. The earlier Balanced ISO also shows the same start-time
behavior. Raw VOB counts and identical elementary hashes establish that
authoring did not discard or alter either payload. This work does not claim a
standalone-player playback test or a fix to FFmpeg's DVD demuxer.

Unit tests, vet, media/engine/process race checks, the dedicated mux regression,
the production PAL/NTSC pipeline and the desktop build passed. Raw evidence and
logs remain in scratch; durable source and this summary remain in the repo.
